What's a stock pair, and what do the labels mean?

A pair is simply two different companies we compare, such as A/B. Their prices don't have to be the same. We're looking for a past relationship that may have drifted apart, not guaranteed profit.

Research candidate means today's strict statistical checks passed, but it is still not a trade recommendation. Preliminary watch means something looks interesting but important tests failed. Not qualified means the scanner hasn't found a sufficiently supported opportunity.

What is the scanner actually doing?

It compares stocks from similar industries using their historical closing prices. It looks for how closely they move together and whether their price relationship has drifted. It automatically checks many pairs and then selects two for a deeper daily review.

The status describes the screening tests only. No label guarantees a profitable trade. Click "Why?" on any row for the measured numbers and short explanations.

Quick explanations

How to read results: choose one question at a time.

What is statistical arbitrage?

A type of trading research that searches for patterns between assets, sometimes hoping that a temporary gap will shrink. Prices might not move back together, and the research here is not evidence of a profitable system.

What does Z-score mean?

Think of a rubber band: the Z-score measures how unusually stretched the *modeled gap* between two stocks is compared with its past variation. A value near 0 is ordinary for this model. Above +2 or below -2 is unusually far from the recent center, but the gap can keep growing.

What does correlation mean?

Correlation measures how similarly the two stocks' daily returns have moved. +1 means they moved together perfectly in the sample, 0 means little linear relationship, and -1 means opposite movements. A high value alone does not mean a pair trade will work.

What is the p-value?

A statistical test of a past long-run price relationship. Smaller values can support that relationship under the test's assumptions. They don't tell you the probability of making money or prove future stability.

What is the q-value?

The scanner checks hundreds of combinations. Some will look interesting just by chance. The adjusted q-value is an extra safeguard for repeated testing. Passing it still doesn't guarantee performance.

What are beta drift and half-life?

Beta describes how the scanner estimates one stock moves relative to the other in log prices. Beta drift checks whether that estimate has changed. Half-life estimates how quickly a past gap tended to shrink in a simplified model; it's not a guaranteed number of days.

What does a historical replay show?

It tests simplified "what if" examples against past closes, including fixed assumed trading costs. These are not real orders, not an independent walk-forward test, and not a forecast. Some past data influenced which pair was chosen.

Can I trade directly from this site?

No. It cannot place orders. It has no real-time exchange-quality quotes, verified short-borrow availability, or proven after-cost trading edge. Use the results as questions to research, not instructions to buy or sell.

Is the live scanner using live stock prices?

The "live" panel shows whether the background scan is running and refreshes that status every two minutes. Pair statistics use the last completed daily data supplied by a third-party vendor, not a streaming live stock-price feed.

How are the results checked?

The scanner keeps its exact input price file with a fingerprint (SHA-256). An independent script recomputes the results, and the site checks its saved report files. This checks arithmetic consistency—not whether the vendor data is perfectly accurate or a trading strategy is profitable. Older reviews without saved input files remain labeled.
