Understand each number

Simple explanations, without overstating what a model can tell us.

Z-score — how far apart?

A Z-score of +2 means the modeled spread is roughly two standard deviations above its recent center. -2 is below it. The pair might not converge, and a large deviation is not automatically a buying opportunity.

Correlation — do they move together?

High correlation means recent daily returns often moved in the same direction. It does not prove that a long-run price relationship exists.

Cointegration p-value — is the spread stable?

A low Engle–Granger p-value provides some historical evidence of a long-run statistical relationship. It is sensitive to sample length, market regime, and corporate events.

Adjusted q-value — how many tests?

Testing hundreds of pairs makes false discoveries more likely. Benjamini–Hochberg q-values account for multiple comparisons. Low q alone still cannot prove trading profitability.

Beta drift and half-life

Beta drift compares fitted hedge slopes in two half-windows; large changes flag instability. Estimated half-life describes the modeled speed of past spread mean reversion, not a guaranteed holding period.

Historical replay — would it have worked?

The site examines non-overlapping examples of a five-session holding rule with assumed next-close entries and fixed fees/borrow. Because pairs were selected using today's data, results are retrospective illustrations, not an independent walk-forward trading proof.
