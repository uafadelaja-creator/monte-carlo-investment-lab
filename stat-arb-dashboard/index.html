Explained without the math jargon

What do these numbers actually mean?

Read this before making any trade decisions

Z

Z-score: how far apart?

A Z-score tells you how unusually stretched the pair's modeled relationship is versus its own history. +2 means stock A is unusually high relative to B; −2 means A is unusually low. It's not a 2% price move and not a profit probability.

r

Correlation: do they move together?

A value near 1.0 means daily moves usually go in the same direction. Near zero means they don't line up consistently. This screen wants above 0.75, but correlation alone does not mean prices must reconnect.

p

Cointegration: is the relationship stable?

This test checks whether the stocks have evidence of a long-run price relationship, rather than simply moving together recently. A p-value below 0.05 is a screening condition, not a 95% chance you'll win.

q

FDR q-value: avoid false discoveries

We tested 18 pairs, so a few low p-values can happen by luck. The adjusted q-value adds a penalty for screening many pairs. Below 0.05 is more convincing research evidence, but still not proof of profit.

β

Hedge ratio: how do you balance the legs?

The model estimates how sensitive stock A's log price has been to stock B. A sensible positive β can help define a long/short pairing. The listed β is not a ready-to-execute share quantity.

✓

What's a favorable setup?

First, you want a stable relationship (low p and q), strong correlation, and a large temporary stretch (|Z| ≥ 2). Then check earnings, news, available shorting, borrowing fees and an out-of-sample test after costs. No pair currently clears all gates.

Z = +2.5

Stock A is unusually high relative to B. If the relationship is valid, a possible hypothesis is short A / long B and see whether the spread narrows. Not an automatic entry.

Z = 0.0

The relative price is near its modeled center. A pairs trader usually sees no new deviation to act on. Returning near zero is sometimes used as a hypothetical exit.

Z = −2.5

Stock A is unusually low relative to B. If the relationship is valid, the opposite hypothesis is long A / short B. That bet can still lose money if the relationship breaks.

How would a real pairs trade work?

  1. Confirm the relationship: The stocks should be economically related and pass predeclared historical stability tests—not just look alike on a chart.
  2. Look for a stretch: A Z-score crossing ±2 is a watch condition. A price move after disappointing earnings could be permanent, not temporary.
  3. Size both legs: One side would be bought (long), the other shorted. Convert a tested hedge ratio to actual exposure using prices, margin and short availability. A $100–$300 options budget may be impractical for a two-leg stock trade.
  4. Define the exit before entering: A research model might close if |Z| falls near 0.5, or invalidate the hypothesis if |Z| expands toward 3, earnings change the relationship, or the statistical test fails. These are model rules—not guaranteed stop prices.
  5. Demand real evidence: Backtest unseen periods, use next-day fills and subtract spreads, commissions, stock-borrow costs and financing. Then paper-trade before putting capital at risk.

Research integrity

Audit and methodology

Verified observations, approximations and missing validations

CHECKED

Historical inputs

18 distinct pair hypotheses were assembled from dated close-price rows in a public GitHub source, with the most recent shared session ending 2026-11-11. Cross-provider spot checks support selected closes, but not the entire series.

CHECKED

Multiple-testing penalty

Benjamini–Hochberg q-values were recomputed across 18 preliminary p-values. Neither pair with nominal p < .05 retained significance at q < .05.

NOT COMPLETED

Price adjustment audit

The source labels the columns “close” and includes optional dividend/split fields. Split-adjusted versus fully dividend-adjusted consistency has not been independently verified. This can materially affect log-price testing.

NOT COMPLETED

Production test parity

The preliminary residual-ADF lag selection and p-value approximation have not been reproduced end-to-end against the full statsmodels Engle–Granger implementation. Values are research estimates.

NOT COMPLETED

Out-of-sample alpha

There is no validated walk-forward net-P&L test for this new candidate universe. Execution timing, bid/ask spread, borrow availability, financing, earnings breaks and hedging drift require separate verification.

DOCUMENTED

Frozen screening parameters

252 completed sessions for log-price OLS; 90 sessions of daily-return correlation; 60 formation residuals for Z normalization. The signal observation is excluded from estimation to avoid look-ahead.

Gate definitions and interpretation

A qualifying research alert would require p < 0.05, return correlation > 0.75 and |Z| ≥ 2 on the same date, plus positive sensible hedge exposure and no material structural break. For defensible model selection, control multiple comparisons and validate the signal on unseen history with costs. A high Z-score measures unusual historical divergence; it does not measure the probability of profit. The exact entry prices and executable hedge quantities are not provided because this dataset is not execution-grade.

* p-values were independently recalculated using statsmodels Engle–Granger (trend=c, autolag=AIC) on the 252-session formation sample; q-values use Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across all 18 hypotheses. These are reproducible statistical estimates, not independently verified adjusted-price or trading-profitability evidence. The November 12 audit corrected earlier numerical estimates. Hedge β is the formation log-price regression slope, not a directly executable share hedge. No live prices, options quotes or automated brokerage trading are embedded.
